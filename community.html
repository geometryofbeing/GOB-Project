The Idea

Built by More Than One Person

Circle, triangle, square didn't stay on the mat. The hope is that they won't stay on this website either.

Right now, the WholeShape is being studied, cultivated, and embodied by the community at Aikido of Champlain Valley — on the mat, and in the lives people carry off it. But it isn't specific to Aikido. The shapes show up in how a nurse handles a hard shift, how a parent sets a boundary, how a team gets through a deadline under pressure.

And there are almost certainly countless people and communities around the world already living as the WholeShape, without ever having called it that. Nothing here was invented. The shapes are older than any of us and belong to no single language, politics, religion, or period of history. What this lens offers is a way to see which qualities are in balance when a person or a community works well — and to name them plainly enough that the naming can travel.

The plan is to build a library rather than a conversation — a place where people write up how they're using the shapes in their own sector, in their own words, and submit it. Business, healthcare, education, art, parenting, sport, whatever it is. Written once, it stays useful to whoever finds it next.

Different people, different rooms — the same three shapes, held at once.
